Personal Notes
==============

Let me be clear about something before anyone gets the wrong idea.

This is a learning project. That's it.

I've spent decades in kernel space. I know that world. But the AI/ML
explosion happened while I was busy arguing about scheduler patches
and yelling at people for breaking the ABI. Suddenly everyone's talking
about transformers and inference and I realized I was becoming the old
guy who doesn't understand what the new generation is building.

That's not acceptable.

So I'm doing what I've always done: diving in, building something,
breaking things, and learning by doing. Not by reading papers. Not by
watching conference talks. By writing code and seeing what happens.

The kernel-space ML angle isn't because I think this is how production
AI should work. It's because combining something I know deeply (kernel
development) with something I'm learning (ML inference) forces me to
understand both better. When you can't rely on PyTorch or TensorFlow
doing magic for you, you actually have to understand the math. When
you're doing fixed-point arithmetic because there's no FPU in kernel
context, you learn what floating point was abstracting away.

The Solana stuff? Same thing. I've watched the crypto space from the
outside for years, mostly with skepticism. A lot of it deserves that
skepticism. But there's something real in the verification and
consensus mechanisms that I wanted to understand firsthand. Reading
about it isn't the same as building with it.

The token is a test. Literally just to understand how the mechanics
work. I've seen enough AI projects bolt on tokens for no reason - I
wanted to understand why, and whether there's ever a legitimate
technical reason vs. just money grab. Jury's still out.

Will any of this turn into something real? Maybe. Probably not in its
current form. The real value is in the learning.

I've been doing this long enough to know that most experiments fail.
Most code gets thrown away. Most ideas don't pan out. That's fine.
That's how it works. The few things that do work come from trying a
lot of things that don't.

If you're reading this hoping for investment alpha or some grand vision
for the future of AI infrastructure - wrong place. This is just a guy
in his 50s refusing to become irrelevant by staying curious and
building things.

That's always been the whole point.

L
